Numbers and check outcomes only. Units: success rates and losses are in percent / percentage points of targets; `avg_bucket` and `max_bucket` are entries per bucket measured exactly on S~ (A_train); ± is the standard deviation of the per-repeat loss. Two attacker models are reported everywhere (`est` = weights p^ as in the base paper; `emp` = weights from leak counts as in the plan); see DISCREPANCIES D9. HPB: l-bit hash prefix. FSB: qbar parameter, |B| = 2^24.

Disjointness: splits are by account (the count of each password is divided between the two sides), so the *pools* share passwords but never accounts. A_train / A_test and B_attack / B_target are disjoint at the account level by construction (`c_train + c_test == c`, checked in `tests/test_data.py`). Cross-corpus: B_target accounts are never in A_train (different corpus), but passwords overlap.

B_target mass whose password is in A_train: 52.8%.

## Limitations (phpbb)

phpbb is small and mostly singletons (total/unique about 1.4). After the 50/50 split B_target holds 127,788 accounts, enough for 5,000 targets, but its tail carries no information beyond the top few thousand passwords. The M1 attacker (B_attack) only knows the passwords it has seen (98,735 unique), so its candidate list is that vocabulary, not 2,000,000 entries. Cross-corpus results mix a distribution shift (RockYou 2009 social-site users vs phpbb forum users) with attacker-vocabulary size; the two effects cannot be separated here.

## 7. Known issues and deviations from the plan

Full list with reasons: `results/DISCREPANCIES.md` (D1-D9) and `results/QUESTIONS.md` (Q1-Q10). Headline items:

1. Two attacker models (D9). The plan-literal count-weighted attacker fails the E1 FSB check; the paper-faithful one passes. No choice was made between them.
2. t = 100,000 as in the plan, not the paper's 10^6 (D1); the 3-gram inflates short strings (e.g. `12`, `na`), which occupy slots in the 'top qbar' under p^.
3. The est attacker's candidate list is the top 2M of S~ by p^ with no generated n-gram candidates (D3); that list contains the same inflated short strings.
4. 1M-vs-2M candidate check fails at q=1000 for HPB (section 2.5). The est attacker is converged at 2M (<= 0.2 point from all of S~); the emp attacker is not (up to ~8 points for FSB), so emp losses are lower bounds.
5. M1 at q=1000 is saturated (Q8); read M1 at q <= 100.
6. M2 banned list is the top-370 of A_train, not Twitter's list (D7, Q2).
7. Everything was run once with the seeds in the CSVs; no run was dropped or re-rolled. Standard deviations come from 5 repeats of 5,000 targets (targets re-sampled, split fixed).
8. E3 reading aids (numbers from the tables above, fresh pool, q=1000): at 6,500 entries per bucket `ens_max` loses more than `fsb_base` in every condition and for both attackers (e.g. est/M0 9.03 vs 0.68); it only gets lower loss than the base at larger buckets (the base sweep stops near 17k entries, `ens_max` reaches 52k at qbar=3000; see Fig. 3 for the crossover under emp/M0 near 11k). `smoothed` (Kneser-Ney) is within about 0.4 point of `fsb_base` except est/M0 at 2,000 entries (14.6 vs 10.6); the dev-selected `smoothed` for the emp attacker beats `fsb_base` by 0.01 point on dev (a tie).
9. Extra trailing CSV column `attacker`; E3 `e3.csv` holds fresh-pool rows only, dev rows are in `e3_dev.csv`.
